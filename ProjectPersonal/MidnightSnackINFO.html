About Midnight Snack

Midnight Snack is a simple, very short comedic horror game made for a Halloween game jam. You play as a guy who wakes up in the middle of the night, feeling hungry and wanting a midnight snack. As you get up, you proceed to explore the home and realize the horrors within — but still proceed to make the sandwich. Finally, as you finish making the sandwich, you need to find one last item, and a cutscene plays where a monster who had the salt gives it to you, because he was using it for his midnight snack too. Yes, the monster was your roommate. Lol.

Purpose of The Project

As the only developer for this game jam, I did the majority of coding and game design. But the goal of the project was learning to use Unity's Cinemachine — to explore Cinemachine and cutscene development during the jam. However, I did develop other systems which are shown below.

Interaction System

This project features a streamlined interaction system focused on environmental immersion. Players can physically push open doors and pick up a single key item—a flashlight. To keep the experience tight and focused, there is no inventory management; picking up the flashlight automatically equips it, allowing the player to instantly navigate dark areas.

Quest System

The game features a simple fetch-quest structure where the player must collect ingredients to make a sandwich. However, subverting player expectations, the final ingredient triggers a cutscene instead of completing the meal—revealing a monster and abruptly ending the game. This designed anti-climax reinforces the narrative twist.

Cinemachine/Cutscenes

For the first time, I integrated Cinemachine to drive narrative moments. The opening cutscene establishes the world as the player rises from bed, while the ending sequence delivers the game's twist—the monster reveal. Animation isn't my strong suit, so the monster moves with an intentional stiffness that, in hindsight, adds an unintentional charm to the horror. This project was as much about learning camera work as it was about accepting creative limitations.
